# Static-versus-dynamic decision matrix

**A = stable invariant**, **B = configurable academic policy/master data**, **C = operational record**. All recommended implementations are proposals. “DB” means Supabase Postgres with RLS and server-side validation; it does not mean a client can freely edit the value.

Configuration belongs in typed tables when identity, relationships, authorization or cross-row constraints matter. Versioned JSON is suitable for validated weights and adapter mappings. Environment configuration is reserved for deployment endpoints, credentials and operational resource limits.

“Source of truth” is not “whatever was most recently uploaded.” Raw imports are evidence; canonical identities and approved requirements control generation. All policy changes create a revision, invalidate dependent draft approvals, and leave published history unchanged.

## Lifecycle and assignment classification

- **Dynamic entities (B/C):** cohorts and their cohort-owned sections are configured as needed; offerings and teacher assignments change over time. No permanent cohort list or fixed section count is an invariant.
- **Configurable policies (B):** admission, progression, graduation, term structures and scheduling rules are approved per applicable department/program and calendar; do not derive progression from batch labels.
- **Lifecycle rules (B/C):** explicitly record cohort-term progression and active, graduated or archived status under approved rules. Archiving must retain historical references and authorized access.
- **System invariants (A):** scoped identity and valid relationships, no resource conflicts for assigned resources, and immutable published snapshots. Later configuration changes or teacher assignment revisions cannot rewrite published history. An unassigned teacher is distinct from an invalid course or an unknown nonblank teacher; the institution must decide the generation/publication gate for pending assignments.
